Memory is an advanced undergraduate and graduate seminar built around primary research. Students read classic and contemporary studies, consider competing accounts of remembering, and ask what the evidence tells us about everyday memory and memory failures.

This seminar places more emphasis on theory, empirical critique, and independent inquiry than the broader Learning and Memory course.

## What Students Study

Topics have included working and semantic memory; retrieval and levels of processing; autobiographical, prospective, and flashbulb memory; metamemory; development and aging; memory disorders; trauma; and false memories. We also examine applied questions in education, clinical practice, and legal settings.

## What Students Do

- **Lead discussions:** Students introduce empirical articles, develop questions, and help classmates examine the evidence.
- **Compare explanations:** Short writing assignments ask students to connect findings across studies and evaluate alternative interpretations.
- **Develop a project:** Students pursue a question in memory science through a research proposal, literature-based project, or another format appropriate to their level.

Representative readings include Atkinson and Shiffrin on memory systems, Baddeley on working memory, Craik and Lockhart on levels of processing, and contemporary research on metamemory, autobiographical memory, and eyewitness evidence.
