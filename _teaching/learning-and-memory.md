Learning and Memory examines how experience changes behavior and how we acquire, retain, and retrieve information. Students move between behavioral experiments, cognitive theories, and the neural systems that support learning and remembering. We also ask what happens when these processes fail and what the research tells us about improving them.

## What students study

- **Learning:** Classical and operant conditioning, prediction error, reinforcement, generalization, discrimination, and transfer.
- **Memory:** Working and long-term memory, different memory systems, encoding and retrieval, implicit learning, and memory errors.
- **Brain and application:** Neural mechanisms, amnesia, and implications for education, health, and legal settings.

## What students do

The course brings experiments into the classroom. Students participate in interactive demonstrations, predict outcomes, and use their observations to evaluate theories. We connect behavioral findings to what is happening in the brain and consider real-world applications.

Students practice retrieval and spacing throughout the semester. Major projects give them room to examine one problem in learning and another in memory in greater depth.

## Interactive labs

I've developed browser-based labs and demonstrations on classical conditioning, blocking, generalization, skill learning, and memory retrieval. They are in different stages of revision. I'm working toward a consistent set of student instructions, instructor notes, and supporting readings for each activity.
