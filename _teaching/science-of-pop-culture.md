Science of Pop Culture is a Flagler Core Curriculum course that uses popular culture as a doorway into social scientific inquiry. In the version of the course I developed, students examine drag, including _RuPaul's Drag Race_, ballroom culture, drag performance, and LGBTQ+ media, as both a pop-cultural phenomenon and a social phenomenon.

The course asks students to think like social scientists. Rather than treating pop culture as “just entertainment,” students use theories and methods from psychology, sociology, gender studies, media studies, and related fields to ask empirical and interpretive questions about identity, performance, representation, stigma, community, and social change.

## Flagler Core

- **Core Literacy:** Social Scientific Inquiry
- **Core Value:** Respectful and Inclusive Community

As a Social Scientific Inquiry course, Science of Pop Culture asks students to apply social science concepts and methods to understand human behavior in cultural context. Students examine how scholars formulate questions, gather evidence, analyze data, and interpret the social meanings of popular media and performance.

As a Respectful and Inclusive Community course, the class emphasizes civil dialogue, perspective-taking, and the dignity of diverse identities and communities. Drag provides a rich case study for examining gender, sexuality, race, class, community, performance, stigma, and joy.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

- Apply principles and methods from the social sciences to address questions, solve problems, or deepen understanding of human behavior.
- Explain how social scientists formulate hypotheses, gather empirical data, and interpret cultural practices.
- Use critical thinking skills to evaluate theoretical approaches to identity, representation, social norms, and popular culture.
- Analyze drag as a cultural and social phenomenon shaped by gender, sexuality, performance, media, power, and community.
- Communicate social scientific ideas in ways that are evidence-based, respectful, and accessible to broad audiences.

## Course Structure

The course is organized like an episode of _RuPaul's Drag Race_, with each module moving students from preparation, to interaction, to individual assessment.

- **Werk Room:** Students begin each module with instructional materials, readings, videos, and guided activities. This is where students build the conceptual and methodological tools they need for the week, including social construction, ethnography, autoethnography, intersectionality, identity, representation, stereotyping, and survey research.

- **Maxi Challenge:** Students then complete a collaborative or discussion-based activity that asks them to apply the week's concepts with peers. These challenges emphasize respectful dialogue, creative thinking, and social scientific interpretation.

- **Main Stage:** Each module concludes with an individual learning assessment. Students engage directly with research, apply findings to new cultural contexts, and demonstrate their ability to analyze popular culture using social science.

## Representative Modules

Modules have included:

- Unpacking gender and drag: the social construction of identity
- Intersectionality and representation in drag
- Social identity, roles, and drag's challenge to norms
- Stereotyping, prejudice, and discrimination in drag and LGBTQ+ communities
- Mainstreaming, policy reactions, and drag in public life

Across these modules, students encounter materials such as _Paris Is Burning_, research on _RuPaul's Drag Race_, scholarship on drag pedagogy, discussions of intersectionality and gender theory, qualitative methods, survey research, and contemporary debates about LGBTQ+ representation and rights.

## Research LaLaPaRuZa

The major integrative project is the Research LaLaPaRuZa. Students develop an essay that functions as a focused social scientific literature review on a topic of their choice. They identify a question, locate peer-reviewed scholarship, synthesize findings, and build an evidence-based argument about popular culture, identity, representation, or social life.

This project asks students to show that they understand not only what they think about a topic, but how they know. They practice finding scholarly sources, evaluating empirical claims, integrating theory and evidence, and communicating social scientific insight clearly.

One student project from this course developed into a published undergraduate article in _Saints Academic Review_, Flagler's undergraduate research journal: Jena Bouchard's “Beyond the Binary: Drag as a Tool for Nonbinary Identity Formation.”

## Teaching Approach

This course blends pop culture analysis, social scientific research literacy, and creative course design.

- **Pop Culture as Data:** Students analyze drag performances, reality television, documentaries, interviews, public debates, and media representation as cultural texts that can reveal broader social processes.

- **Methods in Context:** Students learn about methods such as ethnography, autoethnography, qualitative analysis, survey research, and literature review by seeing how those methods help scholars understand drag and LGBTQ+ communities.

- **Structured Interaction:** Discussion boards and peer replies are framed as Maxi Challenges, inviting students to engage with one another while practicing respectful dialogue across differences.

- **Individual Research Practice:** Main Stage assessments ask students to apply social scientific research to new contexts, encouraging originality, critique, and synthesis.

- **Inclusive Inquiry:** The course asks students to approach drag with curiosity, rigor, and respect, using social science to understand how popular culture can reflect, challenge, and transform social norms.
