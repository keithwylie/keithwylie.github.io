Science of Pop Culture uses drag and popular culture to introduce social scientific inquiry. Students consider what performances, television, and public debates can tell us about identity, representation, inequality, and social change. The course examines drag as a cultural practice through social psychological and interdisciplinary research.

## What Students Study

Modules have addressed the social construction of identity, gender expression, intersectionality, stereotyping, representation, commercialization, community, and political debates around drag. Students also learn to distinguish empirical claims from interpretation and examine how researchers study culture.

## What Students Do

- **Read culture as evidence:** Students analyze performances, media representations, and public discussion alongside relevant scholarship.
- **Practice research literacy:** Through activities called *Research LaLaPaRuZa*, students locate and evaluate scholarly sources and develop evidence-based arguments.
- **Create an integrative project:** Final work connects social scientific research to a question about drag, identity, representation, or public policy.

The course combines independent analysis with discussion and peer response. One student project developed into an article in Flagler's undergraduate journal, *Saints Academic Review*.
