Teaching Areas

I teach across the psychology curriculum, with courses in legal and forensic psychology, research methods, cognition, learning and memory, psychology of language, abnormal psychology, child psychology, pseudoscience, and social-scientific inquiry courses in Flagler’s Core curriculum.

Recent and recurring courses include:

  • Forensic Psychology and Advanced Forensic Psychology
  • Research Methods and Research in Experimental Psychology
  • Psychology of Language
  • Psychology of Pseudoscience
  • Learning and Memory, Cognition, and Child Psychology
  • Science of Learning, Science of Crises, and Science of Pop Culture

Scholarship

My research applies cognitive, social, and quantitative psychology to questions in law and justice. Major areas include eyewitness memory, investigative interviewing, juror decision-making, lie detection, misinformation, language in legal contexts, and how people interpret evidence.

Selected peer-reviewed publications include work in Applied Police Briefings, Journal of Psychological Inquiry, The Arts in Psychotherapy, Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes, Psychology of Language and Communication, International Journal of Developmental Disabilities, Journal of Investigative Psychology and Offender Profiling, Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, Law and Human Behavior, and Advances in Methods and Practices in Psychological Science.

Student Mentorship

I regularly mentor undergraduate and graduate student research. Student collaborations have led to peer-reviewed publications, conference presentations, honors projects, and master’s theses across legal psychology, forensic psychology, developmental psychology, cognition, misinformation, and applied psychological science.

Service and Professional Activity

My professional activity includes service to academic programs, faculty governance, journal reviewing, conference reviewing, student mentorship, and science translation work related to psychology and law.
