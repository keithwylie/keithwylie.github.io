Science of Learning

Science of Learning (COR 134) is a Flagler Core Social Scientific Inquiry course organized around a practical question: What does research on memory and learning tell us about how to study? Students examine retrieval practice, spacing, interleaving, metacognition, and motivation, then consider what the evidence means for their own coursework.

How the Course Works

Students read Make It Stick by Brown, Roediger, and McDaniel alongside empirical articles. In the face-to-face course, most weeks follow a recurring structure:

  • Monday: Introduce a concept and the research behind it.
  • Wednesday: Reflect and Discuss activities that connect research with students’ experiences.
  • Friday: Research Friday, with practice finding, reading, and evaluating primary research, often in collaboration with librarians.

Students also annotate readings, complete short quizzes, and develop a study plan they revisit after receiving feedback. At the end of the semester, they reflect on what happened when they put research-based study strategies into practice.

Research Friday

Research Friday provides repeated practice with scientific literature. Early assignments guide students through the structure of a journal article. Later activities ask them to locate studies independently, compare evidence, and consider how the findings apply to learning in different settings.

Downloadable Research Friday Assignments

These editable Word worksheets correspond to different points in the semester; I'm sharing a selection, so some numbers are missing. You're welcome to use or adapt them. The two note-taking versions illustrate a revision: the Research Brief was a more demanding first draft, while the revised Research Friday worksheet provided more guided practice when I taught the course for a second semester and saw that students needed additional practice at that stage.

  • 02 — Do Students Practice Retrieval? (Karpicke et al.)

    Read an empirical article section by section and connect its findings to study habits.

  • 02 — The Pen Is Mightier Than the Keyboard (Research Brief, original)

    An earlier, more demanding version that also asks students to find and evaluate a follow-up study.

  • 02 — The Pen Is Mightier Than the Keyboard (Research Friday, revised)

    A more guided version focused on understanding the assigned article and applying its findings.

  • 05 — Spacing and Interleaving in the Real World

    Find a study of spacing or interleaving in a field outside psychology.

  • 06 — When Is Difficulty Desirable?

    Find and compare evidence supporting and complicating desirable difficulties.

  • 07 — Reheating Dunning–Kruger's Nachos

    Compare the original Dunning–Kruger research with a more recent study.

  • 08 — Learning Styles

    Evaluate a recent empirical test of learning styles and discuss connections with a partner.

  • 09 — Motivation

    Examine how motivation and learning strategies work together, then synthesize findings with a peer.
